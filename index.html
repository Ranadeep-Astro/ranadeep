Firefly: Synchrotron Radiation Code

Firefly is a publicly available code I developed for modeling synchrotron radiation in astrophysical contexts such as gamma-ray burst (GRB) afterglows and relativistic outflows. It includes features for:

  • Simulating multi-wavelength afterglow lightcurves (radio → X-ray)
  • Modeling relativistic jet dynamics and counterjet contributions
  • Tracking apparent motion across the observer’s sky
  • Exploring parameter degeneracies between geometry and microphysics

GRB 170817A: Counterjet Hypothesis

Summary: GRB 170817A (GW170817) was the first binary neutron star merger observed in both gravitational waves and electromagnetic signals. Its afterglow showed an unexpected X-ray rebrightening at ~1000 days.

Using our Firefly afterglow code, we tested boosted fireball jet models with new observations (~1234 days). While the counterjet scenario is consistent with some aspects, the predicted apparent motion (~2.6c) does not match observations (5.2c–7.5c).

Key Insight: Apparent motion combined with multi-band lightcurves is crucial to break degeneracies between geometry and microphysics in afterglow modeling.

Paper link: Could the Recent Rebrightening of the GW170817A Afterglow Be Caused by a Counterjet?

Long GRB Duration and Photospheric Radius

Summary: Long gamma-ray bursts (lGRBs) arise from relativistic jets in Type Ic supernovae. The Collapsar model links burst duration to central engine activity and jet breakout time, but simulations show deviations from this simple picture.

Our Approach: Using photospheric emission models of jets emerging from Wolf–Rayet–like stars, we calculated bolometric lightcurves for varying engine durations.

Findings:

  • For long engine durations: t90 ≈ teng.
  • For short engines: t90 depends on the photospheric radius, Rph.
  • Derived relation: t90 ≈ teng + α(Rph/c).

Key Insight: This relation helps explain possible low-luminosity lGRBs where duration reflects both geometry and microphysics, not just engine activity.

Paper link: A Novel Relationship Between Gamma Ray Burst Duration And Photospheric Radius

EP250108a/SN 2025kg: Jet-Driven Stellar Explosion

Summary: EP250108a/SN 2025kg is a broad-line Type Ic supernova with a double-peaked optical light curve, linked to an Einstein Probe fast X-ray transient at z = 0.176.

Findings:

  • Spectrum evolves from blue continuum → SN Ic-BL.
  • Explosion parameters consistent with the SN Ic-BL population.
  • Radio and X-ray resemble low-luminosity GRB 060218/SN 2006aj.

Interpretation: Three scenarios were tested. The favored model is a shocked cocoon of a choked jet in extended CSM, consistent with optical, radio, and X-ray data. This model implies CSM mass Me ≈ 0.1 M⊙ and radius Re ≈ 4 × 1013 cm.

Key Insight: EP250108a/SN 2025kg provides a close analog to LLGRB 060218/SN 2006aj, highlighting the importance of early multiwavelength follow-up in constraining massive-star environments.

Paper link: EP250108a/SN 2025kg: A Jet-driven Stellar Explosion Interacting with Circumstellar Material
